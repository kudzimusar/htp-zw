Editorial policy

Accuracy before speed when necessary.

HealthTimes prioritises verified, evidence-based reporting and aims to make complex health information understandable without sensationalising it. Reporting is expected to identify credible sources, distinguish reported fact from analysis, and treat vulnerable people with dignity and respect.

  • Verify material claims before publication.
  • Use recognised health authorities, credible institutions, research and accountable named sources.
  • Clearly distinguish news, analysis, opinion and sponsored/commercial material.
  • Protect privacy where health information or vulnerable sources require it.
  • Disclose significant corrections rather than silently rewriting the record.
Medical information

Journalism is not medical advice.

HealthTimes reporting is for informational purposes. Articles should not be presented as personal diagnosis or treatment guidance. Where a story discusses clinical evidence, medicines or health interventions, the newsroom should explain the source, limitations and relevance of the evidence in plain language.

Reader standard: People seeking diagnosis, treatment or individual medical guidance should consult a qualified healthcare professional.
HealthTimes Intelligence

AI supports navigation and understanding; editors retain accountability.

Ask HealthTimes is designed to help readers find reporting, summarise published stories, explain why developments matter and surface related coverage. It must not diagnose, prescribe medication, provide dosing or impersonate a clinician. In the production platform, AI responses should be grounded in the authoritative HealthTimes content store with provenance, logging and safety controls.

Commercial standards

Advertising is visible, labelled and separate from editorial decisions.

Paid campaigns support the publication but do not determine HealthTimes reporting. Advertising and sponsorship placements should be clearly labelled. Health-related commercial claims should receive appropriate review before publication, and commercial staff do not silently edit newsroom copy.

Corrections

Readers can challenge the record.

Material errors should be corrected promptly and transparently. Every article provides a correction route. Requests can be sent directly to the editorial desk and significant published changes should be noted in the article history.
